🎓 Viva Voce & Interview Defense Guide

1. What is the Core Principle of this Project?

Real images and videos are created by physical optical sensors (light enters a physical glass lens, passes through a Bayer filter, and hits silicon photodiodes, generating Poisson sensor noise and EXIF hardware records). In contrast, Generative AI models (Diffusion models, GANs, Sora, Midjourney) mathematically synthesize pixels using deep neural decoders. These neural decoders leave distinct, invisible mathematical fingerprints: periodic frequency grid checkerboards, compression discrepancies, and temporal flickering.

2. What are the 5 Forensic Pillars Used?

  • 1. Provenance & Metadata: Real cameras write ISO, shutter speed, and lens tags; AI generators either strip EXIF or store prompt text chunks (like PNG parameters).
  • 2. Error Level Analysis (ELA): Recompresses the image at 90% quality; real images degrade uniformly, while AI or spliced deepfakes display distinct compression boundaries.
  • 3. 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT): Computes the frequency magnitude spectrum to identify periodic checkerboard grid artifacts caused by transposed convolution upsampling layers.
  • 4. Noise Residual (PRNU): Evaluates physical silicon sensor grain vs synthetic plastic-like over-smoothing or artificial Gaussian noise.
  • 5. Video Temporal Consistency: Samples frames to measure inter-frame Structural Similarity (SSIM) and motion vector stability to detect generative flickering and morphing.

3. Top 5 Questions You Might Be Asked in Viva:

Q1: Why do Generative AI images have periodic grid artifacts in frequency space? Answer: Generative networks generate images from low-dimensional latent space using upsampling operations such as Transposed Convolutions or Pixel Shuffle. Because kernel filters overlap periodically on the grid during synthesis, they introduce microscopic spatial periodicities. When transformed via 2D FFT, these periodicities manifest as distinct high-frequency spikes and symmetric crosses.
Q2: What is Error Level Analysis (ELA) and what does it detect? Answer: ELA calculates the pixel-wise difference between the original image and a version resaved at 90% JPEG quality. In an authentic photograph, all 8x8 DCT blocks reach a consistent error equilibrium. If an AI image was generated or a face was spliced in, the new region has a distinct error rate that lights up prominently on the ELA heatmap.
Q3: How do you detect AI-generated videos versus real videos? Answer: We analyze frame-to-frame temporal consistency using the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). Real physical cameras follow continuous optical physics. AI video models (such as Sora, Runway, or Deepfakes) synthesize frames in temporal batches, causing microscopic flickering, texture morphing (e.g. background warping, eye/finger jitter), and abnormal SSIM standard deviations.
Q4: What if an attacker strips EXIF metadata from a real image? Answer: Our ensemble model assigns only 15% weight to metadata. If metadata is missing, the system marks metadata as inconclusive and relies on the visual, frequency (FFT), compression (ELA), and sensor noise (PRNU) pillars to reach the final verdict.
Q5: Why is this explainable approach superior to a black-box deep learning classifier? Answer: Deep learning classifiers often overfit to specific training datasets and cannot explain why a prediction was made. Our multi-pillar forensic framework isolates physical signal properties (optics, compression, sensor physics, Fourier harmonics), providing human-auditable evidence for every score.
